01 / Mandate

Several days. Unlevered stocks. Room to wait.

Research several-day, long-only stock opportunities using own-funded settled cash only. No borrowing, margin, leverage, options, short selling, leveraged or inverse products, microcaps or illiquid securities. Holding cash and making no new trade are valid outcomes. The user makes every trading decision and places all orders.

Adapt the useful process lessons of failure analysis: ask how a promising idea can fail, demand evidence for each dependency, record what is unknown, and stop issuing actionable suggestions when the evidence is unreliable. A process lesson from a different market is a hypothesis about better research discipline, not evidence of stock-market profitability.

This book governs the current research process. Older strategy examples and dated calls remain historical records. Their example percentages, time rules and allocations are not newly adopted personal risk limits.

02 / Three separate decisions

A good-looking idea still has to qualify.

Attractiveness

How does the setup compare?

The buying score describes comparative entry attractiveness at a dated reference price, using the unchanged rubric. It is not a probability of profit or a buy instruction.

Eligibility

Is the evidence sufficient?

Identity, price, session, events and liquidity must pass the evidence gates. A high score cannot override a failed gate. Missing critical evidence means score unavailable and no actionable entry suggestion.

Sizing

Can a private plan be supported?

Sizing is not provided on this public market-research page. A research ranking never establishes how much someone can afford to risk.

Private sizing requires separately authorized, verified capital and complete account and open-order coverage, including pending commitments, plus user-confirmed per-trade and total-open-risk limits. Confirm liquidity needs and horizon. Unknown inputs block sizing; do not infer a numerical budget or reuse an old example.

03 / Required research policy

Check the dependencies before scoring.

Apply these gates before publishing a numeric score or actionable entry suggestion. This is an adopted review policy; this documentation release does not establish automatic enforcement. An incomplete item is a recorded gap, not a favorable assumption.

  1. Verify the instrument. Resolve the issuer, security type, listing and symbol from reliable reference evidence. Confirm actual exchange and symbol session, timezone, trading calendar and any halt or restriction relevant to the reference. An identifier match alone is insufficient.
  2. Verify the price and clock. Retain source, observation time, actual quote or trade time, session and known delay. State whether the reference is a trade, official close, indicative ask or book-only observation. A response timestamp does not make an old price current. Unknown delay remains unknown.
  3. Reconcile the price history. Check splits, dividends and other corporate actions; keep adjusted and unadjusted series consistent. Use sufficiently complete trend and benchmark data from matching periods. Do not calculate apparent returns from incompatible references.
  4. Check events and the thesis. Verify earnings timing and material company, filing, regulatory or financing events against primary evidence. Distinguish the date of a headline from the date of the event. Seek contrary evidence and independently check material facts and calculations.
  5. Check implementation conditions. Obtain usable bid/ask and spread evidence, relevant-session liquidity and volume context, and any execution limitations. A displayed last price is not a guaranteed fill. Reassess if price has moved beyond the documented entry case; no arbitrary chase threshold is adopted.
  6. Write a conditional plan. State the horizon, evidence-based entry condition, thesis invalidation, realistic target or review condition, and review deadline. Compare reward with planned downside after realistic costs and with cash and a diversified alternative. A calculated reward multiple alone does not establish an achievable target.

Fail closed on critical gaps. Use score_status: unavailable and a null score with the specific missing evidence. Do not use zero, a carried-forward score or a guessed value. Informational research can explain the gap, but cannot imply entry eligibility. Recheck evidence when a condition changes.

04 / Failure-mode register

What could invalidate the decision?

Each row pairs a failure with evidence needed and the required response. These are review requirements. Automated detection and recovery tests for this full register remain planned.

Stale or missing price

Check: source, trade/quote time, delay and age relative to the claimed session.

Response: withhold a score and entry suggestion if the reference is unusable. Preserve the prior record with its original date.

Wrong symbol or session

Check: issuer, instrument, listing, timezone, exchange calendar and symbol-specific session eligibility.

Response: quarantine the unresolved instrument. Never silently substitute another ticker or treat an after-hours reference as an official close.

Corporate actions

Check: dated issuer/exchange notices and adjustment conventions across the entire price series.

Response: suspend affected trend and return comparisons until reconciled. Append a correction if an earlier analysis was affected.

Earnings and overnight gaps

Check: verified earnings date and time, other material events and whether the planned holding window crosses them.

Response: document the event exposure and withhold an entry suggestion if it cannot be assessed. Model adverse gaps separately; a stop is not a guaranteed loss cap.

Spreads and liquidity

Check: current bid/ask, spread, relevant-session activity and realistic transaction costs.

Response: reject an illiquid setup or withhold eligibility when evidence is missing. Do not assume a fill at the last price or an indicative quote.

Incomplete account or order coverage

Check: separately authorized verification of settled capital and all relevant accounts, holdings and pending orders.

Response: block private sizing and personalized entry advice until coverage is complete. Market research must not imply an account review occurred.

Correlated exposure

Check: common sector, factor, issuer and event drivers, including overlapping open commitments, in a separately authorized private review.

Response: assess the combined downside against confirmed total-open-risk limits. Unknown exposure blocks sizing; different tickers are not proof of diversification.

Feed outage or conflicting sources

Check: source availability, consistent timestamps, coverage and material disagreements.

Response: suspend affected recommendations, report partial or failed coverage accurately, and require fresh verified evidence before resuming. An outage is not a finding of no opportunity.

Misleading or recycled headlines

Check: original filing or issuer statement, event date, context, corroboration and contrary evidence.

Response: do not award catalyst credit from an unverified headline. Mark unresolved material facts as critical gaps.

Detection or delivery failure

Check: actual review completion, publication and coverage evidence separately from intended schedules.

Response: record delays and missed checks. Never invent a run, refresh old evidence timestamps or imply protection between observations.

05 / Capital and failure response

Allocation is not planned stop risk.

Allocation is the amount committed to a position. Planned stop risk estimates the loss between an assumed entry and an invalidation or stop reference, with modeled costs. They are different quantities. Neither is a guaranteed maximum realized loss: gaps, slippage, halts, liquidity and order behavior can produce a larger loss.

A stop order becomes a market order when triggered; its eventual execution price can differ from the stop price. A limit order constrains price but may remain unfilled. The last trade need not be the price available for execution. These order mechanics are described by Investor.gov’s Types of Orders.

Research circuit breakers

Suspend new recommendations when critical market evidence is stale, contradictory or missing; when instrument identity or event risk is unresolved; or when required private capital, order coverage or confirmed risk limits are unavailable. Record the reason, affected scope and evidence needed to resume. Resume only after a fresh review resolves the failure.

A circuit breaker can suspend recommendations. It cannot ensure an exit, cap realized losses, monitor continuously or guarantee timely delivery. No new numeric risk limits, loss-streak rule, chase rule or capital-buffer multiple are adopted by this edition. Risk limits require explicit user confirmation and a recorded versioned change.

07 / Status without overclaiming

Policy, implementation and proof are different.

Adopted policy

Evidence before action

The gates, failure register, manual execution, cash-only scope, privacy boundary and version protocol in this book are adopted research requirements. Their adoption does not prove a return advantage.

Existing implementation

Publication safeguards

The repository has a public-field validator, a local watch-update helper, separate hard/soft timestamps, immutable score records and rubric/cohort fields. These checks validate data structure and preservation; they do not verify market facts or enforce every gate in this book.

Planned validation

Broader failure tests

Stale-feed, wrong-instrument, corporate-action, news-conflict, outage and incomplete-coverage exercises for the full workflow are planned. This release does not claim those tests exist or have passed.

Hypotheses to evaluate

  • More explicit evidence gates may reduce unsupported recommendations while also rejecting some opportunities. Measure both outcomes.
  • Event, liquidity and correlation reviews may make estimated downside more realistic. Their thresholds and predictive value require prospective validation.
  • The unchanged buying-score-v1 ordering may distinguish future outcomes. It has not been established here as a profitable or calibrated model.

Planned exercises should inject each failure, verify that the affected score or recommendation is withheld, verify that prior records and timestamps remain intact, and test recovery with fresh evidence. Record actual results and limitations before changing a status to tested.

08 / Learn without rewriting the past

Keep a prospective, immutable record.

Record the signal or rejection when it occurs: universe coverage, evidence available at the time, missing inputs, gate outcome and reason, original score or unavailable status, rubric/book versions, reference/assessment/publication times, cohort, conditional plan and review horizon. Preserve failures and missed observations alongside favorable signals. Public exports contain only reviewed market-research fields.

Freeze the original before outcomes are known. Append corrections with a new ID, time, reason and link to the original; never overwrite, backfill from later evidence or delete an unfavorable result. Record later outcome observations separately. The existing score history and dated outcome archive remain intact.

Before evaluating a new cohort, specify its horizon, benchmark, cost assumptions, entry/exit observation rules and treatment of missing data. Evaluate all signals, rejections and failures across the fixed universe and all publication windows, including unavailable cases. Report sample sizes, coverage, overlaps, reference-to-publication lag and uncertainty. Avoid choosing winners, convenient dates or thresholds after seeing returns.

Use realistic bid/ask spreads, fees, slippage, execution delay, gaps, potential nonfills and corporate-action adjustments. Compare with a prespecified suitable benchmark over matching periods and with holding cash; leave unsupported comparisons unavailable. Separate provisional, intraday and official-close cohorts and every rubric version. Overlapping several-day observations are not independent trials.

Reference-price changes are descriptive research outcomes, not assumed fills or realized trading returns. This edition presents no fabricated backtest, new performance result or demonstrated edge. Existing dated audits retain their own limitations.

09 / Future update protocol

Every change leaves a trail.

  1. Read the current state. Read the latest main branch, publishing instructions, current watch data and this book. Preserve intervening work. Updating documentation is separate from a market check or automation change.
  2. Propose a specific revision. Record a new version, truthful change date, reason, supporting public evidence, affected rules and expected effect. Label each item adopted policy, hypothesis, implemented mechanism or completed test.
  3. Validate what changed. Record the test method, actual result and limitations, or explicitly “planned / not run.” Repository and page checks establish publication quality, not trading performance. A new risk limit needs explicit user confirmation; never silently import a numerical example.
  4. Preserve the prior edition. Add a new immutable edition under the version archive, retain older editions and their assets, append this changelog, and update the current-reading page. Corrections get a new edition; do not edit a frozen edition in place.
  5. Protect cohort integrity. Rubric changes start a new rubric version and cohort. Keep prior scores, references, timestamps and outcome definitions. Changes to gates or evaluation rules must be dated and distinguishable in subsequent observations.
  6. Review and publish within authorization. Use a public allowlist: approved strategy text, public symbols, evidence links, version/date, status and test summaries. Exclude personal account facts, holdings, balances, quantities, cost bases, personal limits, identifiers, internal notes/paths and private conversations. Review prose as well as fields.
  7. Verify the release. Review the exact intended diff, run repository checks and link/mobile checks, preserve fresh main with a normal fast-forward or expected-SHA lease, and verify the exact commit’s Pages deployment plus the live book version. Record a blocker honestly if any step fails.

Future updates occur when new evidence and an authorized task justify them. This page creates no schedule, promise of continued monitoring or permission to access an account, trade, change automation or publish fresh scores.
